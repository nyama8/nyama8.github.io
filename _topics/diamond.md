Color centers in diamond, such as the silicon-vacancy (SiV) center, are optically active spin defects embedded in a diamond host crystal.
Their long spin coherences and strong interactions with light make them among the most promising qubit platforms for the realization of long-range quantum networks.
However, in order to realize such applications, color centers must be integrated with photonic structures such as cavities and waveguides.
A major challenge is that integrated photonic devices using the diamond host crystal are challenging to fabricate and scale.

We developed gallium phosphide (GaP)-on-diamond as an alternative platform that can be readily scaled for practical applications.
Here the GaP acts as an integrated photonic device layer that sits on the diamond substrate.
Spin defect centers in the diamond couple via the evanescent field of the GaP waveguides and cavities.
GaP is a strong nonlinear optical material with a large band gap and refractive index, and is consequently one of the only materials capable of integrating with diamond in this manner.
Moreover, such nonlinearity enables a wide range of functionalities not available to diamond natively, which are integral to scaling quantum photonics for practical applications.

My research spans from the development of the GaP-on-diamond platform and GaP nonlinear photonics (see [Nonlinear photonic devices and materials](/research/nonlinear/)) to the realization of GaP-on-diamond devices coupled to SiV centers.
